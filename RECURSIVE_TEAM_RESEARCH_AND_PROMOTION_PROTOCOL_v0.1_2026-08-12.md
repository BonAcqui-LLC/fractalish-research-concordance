# Fractalish Recursive Team Research and Promotion Protocol v0.1

**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Status:** CANONICAL PROCESS SPECIFICATION / GOVERNANCE METHOD  
**Scope:** Team research, multi-model prompting, candidate formation, falsification, experiment preparation, and R2R handoff.  
**Authority boundary:** This document governs process. It does not establish any scientific claim merely because the process was followed.

## 1. Governing principle

**Hot assimilation, cold promotion.**

The program may ingest ideas, papers, model outputs, experimental leads, counterexamples, and candidate mechanisms rapidly. Promotion into scientific authority, runtime authority, public claim, or engineering action remains slow, explicit, receipt-backed, and reversible.

The process is not a voting system and not a consensus machine.

> Model agreement is not evidence. Independent convergence can be informative, but only experiments, proofs, reproductions, and correctly scoped source evidence can establish claims.

## 2. Why this protocol is being frozen now

The Fractalish team has been converging toward a recursive research process across Event 006, R2R, adversarial review, Trial by Fire, multi-model research rounds, and the Sequence-Snapshot Divergence program.

A timely external case study sharpened the need to formalize the method: the 2026 solution of Crouzeix's conjecture by Shanmu Jin, reported by Alex Townsend and Anne Greenbaum in SIAM News. Crouzeix formulated the conjecture in 2004; the reported solution appeared in 2026, after roughly twenty-two years as an open problem. The article reports that a decisive theorem emerged during an approximately sixteen-hour autonomous GPT-5.6 Sol run using a branching portfolio, multiple subagents, deliberate resistance to premature convergence, adversarial proof audits, counterexample-driven rejection, and persistence until a complete proof survived checking. The associated work also preserved successive manuscripts, a Lean formalization, and an axiom audit.

This case is recorded here as an **EXTERNAL PROCESS CASE STUDY**, not as evidence for Fractalish theory and not as a process to copy literally.

The lesson adopted is narrower:

> Search can be extremely fast when branching, independence, retained failure, and adversarial checking are abundant. Therefore verification, provenance, and controlled promotion become the scarce resource.

## 3. The canonical research loop

### Stage 0 — Define the question and authority boundary

Before team research begins:

- state the question;
- state what would count as an answer;
- state what is currently known versus proposed;
- identify protected / withheld evidence;
- identify what must not change after reveal;
- define whether the task is exploratory, confirmatory, engineering, proof, or literature synthesis.

Do not smuggle the preferred conclusion into the task definition.

### Stage 1 — Freeze the source packet

Preserve the exact inputs supplied to the research team:

- papers;
- datasets or dataset descriptions;
- prior handoffs;
- relevant code or receipts;
- known claim boundaries;
- the exact research prompt.

Where practical, hash the packet.

### Stage 2 — Independent first-pass research

Send the same core problem to multiple researchers/models **independently**.

Requirements:

- first-pass responders should not see each other's answers;
- do not ask for consensus;
- ask what each would do if they inherited the program;
- require competing explanations and falsifiers;
- preserve model-specific novelty and dissent;
- permit rejection of the project's framing.

The objective is branch generation, not agreement.

### Stage 3 — Force branch diversity

Where the task warrants it, explicitly require genuinely different approaches.

Examples:

- native-domain explanation;
- mathematical/formal reduction;
- adversarial falsification;
- alternate substrate or cross-domain test;
- measurement critique;
- simplest-baseline explanation;
- destructive / reconstruction test;
- prospective unknown-mechanism test.

Do not allow every branch to collapse early onto the most attractive story.

### Stage 4 — Adversarial second pass

Run a second research pass designed to attack the first.

Possible mechanisms:

- expose the broader theory only after the independent first pass;
- ask whether newly introduced concepts are redundant with established theory;
- ask for counterexamples;
- ask what would kill each claim;
- ask whether the apparent mechanism is only incomplete state specification;
- ask whether a simpler native model absorbs the result;
- ask where terminology is functioning as a universal solvent.

This is not a request for politeness or balance. It is a request to find load-bearing failures.

### Stage 5 — Preserve raw outputs and classify the comparison correctly

Never replace the raw research outputs with a consensus summary.

Classify findings into at least three buckets:

1. **Cross-prompt independent convergence** — an idea appears before it was suggested and survives later attack.
2. **Prompt-induced development** — an idea was supplied by the prompt, then operationalized, constrained, falsified, or improved by respondents.
3. **Model-specific novelty / outlier** — an idea supplied by only one or a few researchers that may be valuable precisely because it is not consensus.

Repeated mention of a concept after it was explicitly prompted is **not independent convergence**.

### Stage 6 — Extract candidate claims, not themes

Convert the research into explicit candidate statements.

Each candidate should specify:

- the claim;
- target domain;
- measurable variables;
- directional or numerical prediction where possible;
- strongest conventional/native explanation;
- decisive falsifier;
- contamination / leakage risk;
- required evidence level;
- disposition if the result is negative.

Themes are not promoted. Candidates are.

### Stage 7 — Freeze the native / simpler baseline

Before testing the candidate, specify the best reasonable conventional competitor.

Rules:

- do not invent a weak straw baseline;
- do not permit unlimited post-hoc state augmentation;
- if bounded state augmentation is allowed, freeze the budget and variables before reveal;
- require the candidate to add residual predictive or explanatory content beyond the native baseline.

A new vocabulary that reproduces an existing prediction receives no novelty credit.

### Stage 8 — Trial by Fire

Before spending protected evidence, deliberately attack the candidate.

Attempt to:

- remove assumptions;
- reverse causal ordering;
- construct pathological cases;
- identify hidden external support;
- substitute alternative representations;
- find counterexamples;
- destroy the proposed mechanism while preserving superficial outputs;
- reproduce the output from a simpler mechanism.

Record first failure, last surviving invariant, and whether the structure can reconstruct when only minimal admissible conditions are restored.

### Stage 9 — Freeze the experiment / proof contract

Before outcome reveal or execution, freeze as applicable:

- candidate claim;
- dataset / roots;
- target universe;
- estimand;
- exclusions;
- thresholds;
- randomization procedure;
- native baseline;
- forbidden set;
- expected result structure;
- scorer / verifier;
- environment;
- prompts;
- hashes / receipts.

If an ambiguity is discovered before reveal, amend transparently and create a successor commitment. Never rewrite the historical commitment.

### Stage 10 — Execute once the instrument is frozen

After the pre-reveal gate closes:

- stop theoretical redesign;
- expose the protected evidence;
- run the frozen procedure;
- accept the result.

Do not turn falsification discipline into indefinite avoidance of the experiment.

### Stage 11 — Independent verification

Where possible, require an independent checking route:

- separate implementation;
- separate proof;
- independent formula verification;
- external reproduction;
- orthogonal measurement;
- formal verification;
- source-held-out replication.

Independence is more valuable than repeated execution of the same pipeline.

### Stage 12 — Disposition

Use an explicit disposition vocabulary:

- `MERGE` — evidence supports bounded integration;
- `CONSTRAIN` — some part survives with narrower scope;
- `HOLD` — evidence or integrity is insufficient;
- `REJECT` — the candidate failed under the declared test.

Negative results and failed branches remain canonical.

### Stage 13 — R2R handoff

Only after a research candidate is adequately formed does it enter governed R2R promotion control.

Current R2R semantics remain authoritative:

- `register-source`
- `open-candidate`
- `freeze-baseline`
- `plan`
- `run`
- `record-review`
- `disposition`
- `issue-commit-certificate`
- `promote` (receipt only; not deployment)
- `verify-receipt`
- `verify-chain`
- `checkpoint-concordance`

The recursive team-research protocol is therefore the **pre-candidate formation and challenge layer**; R2R is the **governed promotion-control layer**.

Neither layer independently authorizes deployment or runtime effect.

## 4. Non-negotiable anti-drift rules

1. Preserve the original prompt and raw outputs.
2. Preserve failed branches.
3. Preserve dissent.
4. Do not convert consensus into evidence.
5. Do not count prompt-induced repetition as independent convergence.
6. Do not weaken the native baseline after seeing results.
7. Do not add indices, operators, exclusions, or thresholds post hoc to rescue a claim.
8. Do not silently replace historical artifacts with cleaned-up successors.
9. Separate exploratory learning from confirmatory adjudication.
10. Separate scientific establishment from engineering authorization.
11. Treat `HOLD` as a valid and often desirable outcome.
12. When generation becomes abundant, spend resources on verification and organization rather than producing redundant prose.

## 5. Research prompting standard

A strong team prompt should usually ask some form of:

> If you inherited this program, what would you do to learn the most?

And require:

- what are we missing;
- what is the simpler explanation;
- what would falsify this;
- what can be tested now;
- what must wait;
- what is the single strongest next experiment;
- what is the single strongest falsification experiment;
- what opportunity are we most likely failing to see.

When useful, follow with a second prompt that exposes the expanded theory and asks the team to attack its redundancy, universality, and predictive value.

## 6. Planner / time-management integration

The future rolling planner should represent this protocol as bounded work states, not as an invitation to keep every research lane active.

Suggested planner states:

`INTAKE`  
→ `INDEPENDENT RESEARCH`  
→ `ADVERSARIAL REVIEW`  
→ `CANDIDATE EXTRACTION`  
→ `BASELINE / FALSIFIER FREEZE`  
→ `TRIAL BY FIRE`  
→ `PREREG / CONTRACT FREEZE`  
→ `EXECUTION`  
→ `VERIFICATION`  
→ `DISPOSITION`  
→ `R2R PROMOTION CANDIDATE`  
→ `CONCORDANCE CHECKPOINT`

WIP limits should prevent multiple speculative lanes from simultaneously advancing into execution.

## 8. Relationship to Sequence-Snapshot Divergence, ANF, Natural Math, and UMRM

This process is compatible with those research lanes but does not establish them.

- Sequence-Snapshot Divergence supplies a candidate root assay.
- ANF supplies hypotheses to attack.
- Natural Math supplies unusually constrained local-agent test beds.
- UMRM supplies prospective unresolved edges where the grammar can be wrong before mechanism is known.
- R2R governs promotion after a candidate has survived formation and challenge.

## 9. Core compression

If only a few lines survive, preserve these:

> **Branch widely. Keep the branches independent. Make failure cumulative. Attack attractive answers before promoting them. Freeze the contract before protected evidence. Preserve every receipt. Promote slowly.**

> **Hot assimilation, cold promotion.**

> **When generation becomes abundant, epistemic integrity becomes the scarce resource.**
